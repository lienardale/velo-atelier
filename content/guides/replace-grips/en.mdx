<Step id="remove-grips">

First take out the bar-end plugs if they are separate from the grips. A lock-on grip comes off easily: undo the bolt on each lock ring by a few turns with the <Tool id="allen-keys" alt="multi-tool" />, then pull the grip off while twisting it.

A plain push-on grip is held by friction. Slide the blade of a <Tool id="flat-screwdriver" alt="multi-tool" /> under its edge, without scratching the bar, pour a little <Tool id="isopropyl-alcohol" /> into the gap and twist: it slides off. A grip you are throwing away can also be slit with a <Tool id="utility-knife" />, blade flat and pointing away from you, never on a carbon bar, which a cut weakens.

</Step>

<Step id="clean-bar">

Wipe the end of the bar with <Tool id="rags" /> dampened with isopropyl alcohol, to remove the glue, rubber and dirt the old grips left behind. Avoid oil, soap and anything greasy: whatever stays slippery will make the new grips turn.

Then look at the bar where the grips used to hide it. A crack, a gouge, corrosion or, on a carbon bar, a chip or a crush mark left by a clamp means it must be replaced before you ride. Check too that the end of the tube is smooth, with no burr: a sharp edge tears the new grip as it goes on.

</Step>

<Step id="fit-grips">

Position an ergonomic grip before fixing it: its wing should support your palm in line with your wrist, hands in the riding position. Slide the lock-on grip on to the end of the bar, leaving a few millimetres between its lock ring and the brake lever or shifter, then tighten the bolt to the torque printed on the lock ring or given by the maker, often 2 to 4 N·m, with the <Tool id="torque-wrench" />: these small bolts snap or mark the bar if you force them.

A push-on grip goes on with its inside wet with isopropyl alcohol: slide it on in one go while twisting, line it up at once, then let it dry for at least an hour.

</Step>

<Step id="secure-check">

Take each grip in your whole hand and try hard to twist it, then to pull it outwards: it must neither turn nor slide. A lock-on grip that still moves needs a cleaner bar, never more than the specified torque; a push-on grip that moves once dry must be fitted again.

Check that each end of the bar is closed, by the grip or by a plug. Finally, check that the brake levers and shifters have not moved and still fall under your fingers, then ride at walking pace before riding normally.

</Step>
