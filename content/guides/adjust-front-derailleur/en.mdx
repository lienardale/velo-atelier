<Step id="height-angle">

Put the bike in the <Tool id="work-stand" /> and shift onto the big chainring, or push the cage over it by hand. From the side, the bottom of the outer cage plate should clear the teeth of the big chainring by 1 to 3 mm. From above, that plate should run parallel to the chainring, or follow the maker's mark when there is one.

To correct it, loosen the clamp bolt around the seat tube, or the bolt holding the derailleur on the frame's brazed-on tab, with the <Tool id="allen-keys" alt="multi-tool" />. Move it, then tighten with the <Tool id="torque-wrench" /> to the maker's torque, often 5 to 7 N·m. On a direct mount, the height is fixed.

</Step>

<Step id="limit-screws">

Find the L and H marked by the screws, whose positions vary: L stops the cage on the small-chainring side, H on the big-chainring side, and screwing one in limits travel. Undo the cable bolt with the <Tool id="allen-keys" alt="multi-tool" />; with the chain on the small chainring and largest sprocket, set the L screw with the <Tool id="phillips-screwdriver" alt="multi-tool" /> so the inner plate just clears the chain, about 0.5 mm.

On the smallest sprocket, turn the crank slowly with one hand and push the derailleur body, not the cage, outwards with the other until the chain climbs onto the big chainring; if it will not, back H out. Hold it at its stop and set H: 0.5 to 1 mm to the outer plate.

</Step>

<Step id="cable-tension">

Go back to the small chainring. Screw the barrel adjuster on the shifter, or the inline one on the housing for drop bars, all the way in, then back it out one turn (some recent road derailleurs have their own tension bolt instead: follow their manual). Pull the cable with the <Tool id="pliers" /> and clamp it in its groove with the <Tool id="torque-wrench" />, to the maker's torque, often 5 to 7 N·m.

Shift onto the big chainring. If the chain hesitates to climb, unscrew the barrel adjuster a quarter turn to add tension; if it drops back reluctantly, screw it in. On a triple, set the tension so the inner cage plate just clears the chain on the middle chainring and the largest sprocket.

</Step>

<Step id="test">

Turn the pedals and go through every combination: each chainring with each sprocket, in both directions. Shifts should be crisp, and the chain must never drop inside the small chainring or over the big one. A slight rub can remain in the cross-over combinations; if your shifter has a half-click trim position, it is there to remove it.

If the chain drops towards the frame, screw the L screw in a quarter turn; if it goes over the big chainring, screw the H screw in. Then take a short ride somewhere quiet, easing off the pedals for each chainring shift: a shift that stays slow needs a little more cable tension.

</Step>
