<Step id="inspect-rim">

Spin each wheel slowly and look at the braking track, the smooth band the blocks rub on, on both sides. Many rims carry a wear indicator, most often a fine groove all the way round the track: as long as you can see it, there is metal left. Others have a small hole that, depending on the maker, either disappears or appears once the rim is worn: check the maker's instructions.

Then run a finger over the track: it should be flat and smooth.

<Warning level="danger">

A sidewall that is dished, cracked or bulging, or a wear indicator that shows wear, means a rim worn too thin that can burst under tyre pressure. Stop here: have the rim or the wheel replaced at a workshop.

</Warning>

</Step>

<Step id="clean-rim">

On a very dirty rim, first wash the wheel with soapy water, rinse and dry it. Then dampen <Tool id="rags" /> with <Tool id="isopropyl-alcohol" /> and wipe the braking track on both sides while turning the wheel, until the rag no longer turns black. Move to a clean part of the rag often: a rag that is already black only spreads the grime around.

Use no polish, no wax, no lube and no cleaner that leaves a greasy film: the track must stay bare. Keep the chain lube bottle away from the wheels too. Hard marks that will not shift come off with the <Tool id="brush" /> dipped in alcohol. On a carbon or ceramic-coated rim, nothing abrasive on the track.

</Step>

<Step id="clean-blocks">

Open the brake to reach the blocks: lift the quick-release lever of a road caliper, squeeze the arms of a V-brake and pull the "noodle" out of its holder, or unhook the straddle cable of a cantilever. Scrub each block with the <Tool id="brush" alt="rags" /> and a little alcohol.

Then look closely at the rubber, with a torch. Pick out the small embedded metal chips with the tip of a toothpick or a knife: they score the rim at every stop. A hardened, shiny surface comes back to life with a light rub of <Tool id="sandpaper" />, held flat. If the wear grooves have almost gone, or the rubber is cracked, replace the blocks.

</Step>

<Step id="test-brakes">

Close the brake again, the most common and most dangerous thing to forget: put the noodle or the straddle cable back, or flip the quick-release lever shut. Squeeze each lever hard: it must turn firm well before the bar. Spin each wheel: no block should rub or touch the tyre.

Push the bike while squeezing one lever, then the other: the wheel must lock. Then brake a few times in a quiet street, at low speed first. The first stops may still squeal while the blocks finish cleaning themselves on the rim. A squeal that persists means the blocks need toeing in; a brake that still slips means they need replacing.

</Step>
