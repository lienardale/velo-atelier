<Step id="identify-sides">

Each pedal has its own side. The right one screws into the crank arm on the chain side, with a standard thread; the left one into the other crank arm, with a reverse thread. So they are not interchangeable, even when they look alike.

Look for the letter stamped on the axle or on its flats, R for the right and L for the left, on the old pedals as on the new ones; with no mark, the instructions will say. A pedal offered to the wrong side does not catch the thread. Check the size of the new ones too: 9/16″ on almost every adult bike, 1/2″ on one-piece cranks and many children's bikes.

</Step>

<Step id="remove-pedals">

The rule holds on both sides, despite the opposite threads: to loosen, the top of the wrench moves towards the back of the bike; to tighten, towards the front. Seen from outside the bike, the right pedal therefore unscrews anticlockwise and the left one clockwise.

Set the crank arm forward. Fit the <Tool id="pedal-wrench" alt="allen-keys" /> on the 15 mm flats of the axle, or the 6 or 8 mm Allen key into the axle from the inside face of the crank arm. Hold the other crank arm and give a sharp push. To use your weight, point the handle towards the back and push down: it is the same direction of rotation.

</Step>

<Step id="grease-threads">

Wipe the thread of each crank arm with <Tool id="rags" /> and look at it: it must be clean, with no stripped thread and no aluminium shavings. A damaged thread will not hold a pedal any more; a shop can sometimes recut it with a tap or fit an insert. Wipe the axles of the new pedals too.

Coat the threads of each pedal with a thin layer of <Tool id="grease" />, or anti-seize paste. It stops the steel axle seizing in the aluminium crank arm, and makes the next removal easier. If a washer comes with the pedals or the crankset, fit it as the instructions say.

</Step>

<Step id="fit-pedals">

Start each pedal straight in its crank arm and screw it in by hand for several turns, the top of the axle moving towards the front of the bike: it must go in without effort. If it resists from the very first turn, take it out again: it is crooked, or on the wrong side.

Then tighten to the maker's torque, often around 35 to 40 N·m, with the <Tool id="torque-wrench" /> and a 6 or 8 mm bit if the axle takes one. Without a torque wrench, 35 N·m is a push of about 12 kg on the end of a 30 cm wrench. Spin the pedals: they must turn freely, without rubbing on the crank arm.

</Step>
