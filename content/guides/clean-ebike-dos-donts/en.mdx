<Step id="remove-battery">

Switch the system off with the button on the display or the battery. If the display comes off, remove it too and keep it somewhere dry. Unlock the battery with its key and lift it out of its housing with both hands: it weighs several kilos and must not be dropped.

Put it somewhere dry, at room temperature, away from the bucket and the hose. Close the cover of the charging port. The empty housing now has its contacts exposed: no water may get in there. If the battery cannot come out without tools, leave it in place, with the system off, and wash around it even more gently.

</Step>

<Step id="wash-gently">

Wash the bike from top to bottom with a sponge and warm soapy water, or with a gentle hose. Never aim water at the motor, the display, the controls, the connectors or the battery housing, and never submerge any part. A pressure washer is out of the question: it forces water past the seals and into the connectors.

On the motor casing, the display and the controls, use only a damp, well wrung-out sponge, then a dry rag. Clean the chain and the drivetrain as on any other bike, keeping the degreaser away from the motor and its cables.

</Step>

<Step id="clean-contacts">

Look at the contacts on the battery and in its housing, on the frame or the rear rack. Brush away dust and grit dry, with a small paintbrush or the <Tool id="brush" alt="rags" />, then a dry rag. Never a wet rag and never a metal tool: a scratched or damp contact conducts poorly, and a metal object across two terminals causes a short circuit.

Look for green or white deposits, blackened marks or a bent pin. At any of these signs, do not refit the battery, and have the bike checked by the dealer. Also check that the key turns and the lock opens without force.

</Step>

<Step id="dry-refit">

Dry the whole bike with clean <Tool id="rags" />, especially around the battery housing, the connectors and the display, then let it air-dry until no trace of moisture is left. A single drop of water between two contacts is enough to cause a fault.

With the housing fully dry, slide the battery in until it clicks, lock it and remove the key. Pull on it: it must not move. Refit the display, switch the system on and check that the charge level shows with no error message. Squeeze each brake, then ride a few metres: the assistance should cut in from the first pedal strokes.

</Step>
